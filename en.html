01The island

Forest down to the sand, clear-water coves and a fishing village

Saboga sits in the Gulf of Panama, in the Las Perlas Archipelago, across from Isla Contadora. Its village of about 700 residents lives from artisanal fishing and services.

The rest is coast and forest: light-sand beaches, coves between rock slabs and tropical forest down to the water. The scale is small, the island is walked on foot, and it is preserved through low-density development.

02Why Saboga?

What other islands promise, here already exists

Four conditions an international buyer checks before the view: title, power, community and environment.

Title

Titled land

Each parcel is a property (finca) recorded at Panama’s Public Registry, with its own number, survey plan and owner. It is not a possessory right (untitled occupation, common on islands and coastlines): registered title allows you to convey, mortgage and bequeath with full legal certainty.

Power

Grid in service

A 3.6 MW plant in four gensets, which also supplies Contadora through a submarine cable. Distribution by ENSA (EPM Group).

Community

Village and pier

About 700 residents, a pier and internal paths: local labor and a point of arrival for materials and visits, with no infrastructure left to build.

Environment

Protected sea

Saboga Wildlife Refuge (2024): 8,298 ha, almost all marine, with nesting by four turtle species, a hammerhead shark nursery and humpback whales passing from July to October.

06Getting there

One morning from Panama City

Saboga is in Panama’s Pacific, 64 km from the capital and across from Isla Contadora, which has an airstrip and a ferry. About one hour by air or one and three-quarter hours by sea; the last leg is by boat.

  1. 1 hBy air · approx.

    Panama City to Isla Saboga. A 27 min flight from Albrook to Contadora, transfer to the pier and a 5 to 10 min boat ride.

  2. 1 h 45By sea · approx.

    Panama City to Isla Saboga. A 1 h 30 ferry to Contadora (up to five weekly departures depending on season) and a 5 to 10 min boat ride.

  3. 5 to 10 minBoat

    Contadora to Isla Saboga. Less than 3 km between piers, the same leg for visits and materials.

  4. 64 kmDistance

    In a straight line from the center of the capital. Half a morning door to door.

08Frequently asked questions

What international buyers ask before deciding

General guidance, current as of publication. Every transaction is reviewed with a Panamanian attorney before signing.

Can a foreigner acquire land on Saboga?

Yes, on the same terms as a Panamanian citizen and with no residency requirement. The only constitutional restriction covers a 10 km strip along the land borders with Costa Rica and Colombia, far from the Gulf of Panama. You may buy personally or through a Panamanian or foreign company; many buyers use a corporation or a private interest foundation for estate planning.

Do the parcels have registered title?

Yes. They are properties with a registration number at Panama’s Public Registry, not possessory rights. The difference is decisive on an island: only titled land can be mortgaged, inherited and sold with full legal protection. Before signing you receive the current registry certificate, the survey plan and the property’s lien status.

Is the beach in front of my parcel private?

No. On Panama’s Pacific coastline the first 22 meters from the high-tide line are public and cannot be titled (Law 80 of 2009). The parcel begins where that strip ends, and the ocean frontage we indicate is the length of its boundary facing the coast.

What is the purchase process and how long does it take?

Four steps. A written reservation of the chosen parcel. Due diligence on title, boundaries and easements, together with verification of the buyer and the source of funds. A promise-to-purchase agreement when payment is staged. A public deed before a notary and registration at the Public Registry, which usually takes about two business weeks. Our team coordinates each step with both parties’ attorneys.

Why are prices not published?

Each parcel differs in area, ocean frontage, topography and access, and the price is set with the owner according to the project the buyer has in mind. When we receive your request we send you, in writing, the fact sheets of the parcels that fit your profile, with survey plan, area, price and payment terms, and we confirm availability at that moment. This avoids outdated public lists and conversations without context.

What taxes and costs apply to the purchase?

As a general rule in Panama, the seller bears the real estate transfer tax (2%) and the capital gains advance (3%), both settled before the deed. The buyer bears notary and registration costs and their own legal fees. Exact terms are set in each contract.

What does it cost to hold the land each year?

Panama’s property tax is progressive (Law 66 of 2017). For a property that is not a primary residence, assessed value up to B/.30,000 is exempt and the excess is taxed between 0.6% and 1% by bracket. For an unbuilt parcel the annual burden is moderate. The exact figure depends on the registered assessed value and is confirmed with a tax advisor.

Is financing available to foreign buyers?

Panamanian banks lend to foreigners with verifiable income, though with a larger down payment than for nationals and more caution on island land. In Las Perlas most land purchases close in cash or with a payment plan agreed with the seller. We discuss it case by case.

Can the purchase lead to residency in Panama?

It can be the basis of an application. The Qualified Investor Visa grants permanent residency with a minimum real estate investment that the authorities have set between USD 300,000 and USD 500,000 depending on the period, held for five years. Other routes exist, such as the Friendly Nations visa, with lower thresholds. The current amount is confirmed with an immigration attorney before structuring the purchase.

What permits does construction on the island require?

A project on Saboga goes through the Ministry of Environment (an environmental impact study according to the category of the works), the Municipality of Balboa (construction permit) and the utility entities. No building on mangroves or within the public beach strip. The turnkey service includes full management of these permits with licensed professionals in Panama.

How do materials and labor reach the island?

Materials travel by sea from Panama City and are landed at the island pier; marine logistics are planned project by project. Labor combines people from Saboga’s village with crews from Contadora and the capital. Island construction time and cost are estimated with that logistics included from the first budget.

What services does the island have: power, water, connectivity?

Saboga has a working power grid, with its own generation and an interconnection with Contadora. Water is solved per project with rainwater harvesting, wells and storage, as is usual in the archipelago, and each parcel’s fact sheet details its situation. Connectivity combines mobile coverage and satellite links.

When is the best time to visit?

The dry season runs from December to April, with clear skies and calm seas. From July to October humpback whales cross Panama’s Pacific, with sightings in Las Perlas. Visits are arranged year-round with transfer from Contadora.

In what currency, and how is payment made?

In US dollars, legal tender in Panama. Payments are channeled by bank transfer to escrow or notary accounts. Under Panama’s anti-money-laundering rules (Law 23 of 2015), the buyer’s identity and source of funds are documented before closing.
